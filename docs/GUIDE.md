# Folio guide

**English** · [العربية](GUIDE.ar.md) · [Back to the README](../README.en.md)

How each part of Folio works. To install it, see the [README](../README.en.md).

## Install it as a window of its own

Folio opens in a browser tab. In Chrome or Edge you can also install it as an app with its own window and Dock or taskbar icon: open Folio, then use the install icon at the right end of the address bar (or the ⋮ menu → **Cast, save and share → Install page as app**). Folio still has to be running, so open it from the Folio icon first.

## Where your files live

By default everything is in `~/ResearchPlatform` (on Windows `C:\Users\<you>\ResearchPlatform`). Set `RP_DATA_DIR` to use another folder.

```
ResearchPlatform/
  settings.json          your API keys (readable only by you)
  projects/<project>/
    project.json
    notes/*.md           your notes, as plain markdown
    knowledge/           the knowledge base: an Open Knowledge Format bundle
                         (index.md, log.md, papers/*.md, concepts/*.md)
    canvas.json          the notes canvas: cards, links, themes and your drawings
    references.json      your reference library (CSL JSON), with references.bib
                         kept next to it for LaTeX, Zotero or pandoc
    sources/             your PDFs and other sources, their text, a small record for each,
                         and your highlights (<id>.highlights.json, <id>-assets/*.png)
    chats/*.json         chat history
    latex/               your LaTeX paper (main.tex and any other files); the PDF is in latex/.build/
    organize.json        starred items and sidebar folders (files never move)
    thesis.json          your thesis plan: chapters, goals, dates, milestones
    matrix.json          the literature matrix
    flashcards.json      your flashcards and when each is due
    reading.json         your reading list and the page you reached in each PDF
    data/                data files (CSV) you made figures and tables from
    figures.json         your figures and tables, with figures/*.png and *.svg
    supervisor/          drafts your supervisor commented on, and your progress on each comment
    exports/             Word, PDF and other files you exported
    .history/            earlier versions of your notes and LaTeX files
  .tools/                Tectonic, if Folio downloaded it for you
  .trash/                deleted notes, sources and projects, so nothing is lost
  backups/               daily backups of each project (unless you pick another folder)
```

## Backups and history

- **History**: Folio keeps a copy of each note, and of each LaTeX file, every 10 minutes while you write (the last 60). Press the clock button at the top right of a note or of the LaTeX tab, pick a time to read that version, and **Restore this version** to bring it back. The text you replace stays in the list.
- **Backups**: every day, Folio zips each project that changed into a backup folder and keeps the last 10 of each. In **Settings → Backups** choose how often (every day, every week or off), press **Back up now**, or change the folder. Pick a folder that Dropbox, iCloud Drive, OneDrive or Google Drive syncs, and your backups are kept off this computer too. API keys are never in a backup.
- **Restoring**: **Settings → Backups → Restore…** lists the backups in the folder. **As a copy** adds the backup as a new project next to yours; **Replace** puts the backup in place of the project and moves the current one to the trash. **Restore from a .zip file** takes a backup from anywhere.
- **Another computer**: on the first computer, pick a synced folder for backups (or use **Download a backup** in the project's `…` menu). On the second, install Folio, choose the same synced folder in **Settings → Backups** and restore the project. Work on one computer at a time and back up before you switch: a backup is a copy, not live sync.

You can also copy this whole folder yourself.

## Privacy

Folio only listens on your own computer (`127.0.0.1`) and refuses requests from other websites. Your files never leave your machine, except the text you send to the AI provider you chose when you use an AI feature. Finding papers, the first pandoc download, DOI lookup, title search, web and YouTube sources and uncommon citation styles need the internet; everything else works offline.

## Guide

- **Projects**: one per paper or research question.
- **Sidebar**: click **Starred**, **Sources** or **Notes** to fold the list away. Choose **Star** in a source or note's `…` menu to pin it to **Starred** at the top. Hover over Sources or Notes for the folder button to make a folder; drag sources or notes onto a folder (or use **Move to** in the `…` menu), and use a folder's `…` menu for subfolders, renaming or removing it. Folders only arrange the sidebar: your files stay where they are, and removing a folder keeps everything that was in it.
- **Sources**: press **+** next to Sources in the sidebar to add a PDF, Word (.docx), Markdown or text file, a web article, a YouTube video (its transcript), a direct link to a PDF, or pasted text. You can also drop files onto the window. Each source opens on the left with its own note on the right (drag the divider to resize): PDFs in the PDF viewer, everything else in a clean reader, with the video player above a YouTube transcript (click a timestamp to play from there). Switch to **Chat** to ask about that source: the model reads its text, cites page numbers or timestamps, and the conversation is saved with it. **Summary** gives AI reading notes for the source (what it found, key claims with page links, methods, limitations, open questions); they are the source's page in the knowledge base, so chat reads the same notes. Web pages that need a login, or that load their text with scripts, can't be fetched; paste their text instead. Folio reads each PDF's text with [pdf-inspector](https://github.com/firecrawl/pdf-inspector), which keeps two-column papers in reading order, joins lines into paragraphs and keeps headings and tables, so search, chat and summaries see the paper the way you read it (on Intel Macs, where it isn't available, Folio uses its older reader). It also spots scanned pages, and the bar above the note says which pages they are. Press **Read with OCR** there and pick the languages (English, Arabic, French and others, up to four at once): Folio reads the words on those pages on your computer, a few seconds a page, so chat, search, summaries and flashcards can use them. The first time, it downloads each language once (1 to 4 MB, from the Tesseract project); after that it works offline, and new scanned PDFs are read by themselves in the languages you used last. To highlight on a scanned page, use **Capture area**, since the PDF itself still has no text to select.
- **Highlights**: select text in a PDF and pick a color. The highlight stays on the PDF and the quote is copied with a small `p. N` link; paste it into the note, and click the link to jump back to that spot. **Capture area** grabs a figure or table as an image the same way. The **Copy / Add to note** switch above the PDF decides whether new highlights are copied (the default) or go straight into the note. Click a highlight to recolor it, copy it, add it to the note, or remove it. A new color also changes the dot on that highlight's links in all your notes, so your color code stays the same everywhere. Drag the handles on an image in a note to resize it; the size is kept in the markdown as `![caption|320](...)`. Highlights live in their own file; your PDF is never changed.
- **Notes shared across papers**: each paper has its own reading note, but you can also write about a theme while you read several papers. Open the arrow next to **Note** in a paper and choose **Use another note too…**, then pick a note or type a name for a new one. Switch between the paper's own note and its shared notes from the same menu; highlights you add go into the note that is open. A shared note lists the papers it's written with at the bottom, and opening one shows it beside that note. **Stop using … here** takes the note off a paper (the note stays).
- **Canvas**: write one thought per card in the box at the bottom. Pick its kind in the row above the box (Question, Claim, Evidence, Idea…), or leave it on Auto and the AI sorts it; typing `#question` etc. also works. A few seconds after you stop, the AI reads new cards: it sets a kind, adds a short note (a caveat, a definition or a question), tags it, links related cards (green supports, red dashed contradicts; hover a line for why) and lists emerging themes on the right. Double-click a card to edit it; select it and press Delete to remove it (Undo brings it back). It uses the model you last picked in chat and never writes text for your paper.
- **Drawing on the canvas**: the tools on the left draw rectangles (R), ellipses (O), arrows (A), freehand pen strokes (P) and text (T); V or Escape goes back to selecting. Hover a card or shape to see its dots; drag from a dot onto another card or shape to connect them with an arrow that stays attached when they move (the arrow tool does the same). Drag across empty canvas to select several items (Shift or ⌘/Ctrl-click adds one); scroll, Space-drag or middle-drag moves around, and pinch or ⌘/Ctrl-scroll zooms. A small bar above the selection has color, duplicate (⌘/Ctrl D) and delete. Select a shape to resize it; double-click a rectangle or ellipse to label it. Rectangles sit behind cards, so you can use them to group cards.
- **Knowledge base**: the AI's memory of the project, following Google's Open Knowledge Format and Karpathy's LLM-wiki loop. Each PDF you add is read once (automatically if a model is set up) and filed into a page for the paper plus shared concept pages, with page citations and a **Tensions** section when papers disagree. **Check** looks for contradictions, gaps and broken links. Pages are AI notes until you **Mark reviewed** (the OKF `verified` field); you can edit any page. The chat reads the index and the most relevant pages, so it builds on what you've read.
- **Notes**: a markdown editor with a slim toolbar on top (turn into heading, quote or code block; bold, italic, strikethrough, code, highlight, link; bulleted, numbered and check lists; undo/redo). Markdown shortcuts work too: `# ` for a heading, `- ` for a list, `> ` for a quote, `**bold**`, `==highlight==`. The `</>` button switches to the raw markdown. A note's file is named after its first `# heading` and follows it as you change it; renaming the note yourself (click its name at the top) stops that.
- **Exporting to Word or PDF**: in a note, press the download button at the top right; in **Thesis**, press **Export thesis** to join every chapter that's linked to a note, in order, each on a new page, with a title, your name and a table of contents. Pick Word, PDF, OpenDocument, Web page or LaTeX and a citation style: every `[@key]` becomes a formatted citation, and a reference list of the works you cited goes at the end. If a key isn't in your References, Folio tells you which. Captured figures come along; the little links back to highlights are left out. Exporting uses [pandoc](https://pandoc.org): Folio uses yours if it's installed, or downloads it once (about 35 MB) when you first export. PDF goes through LaTeX, so set that up in the LaTeX tab first (one click), or export to Word and save as PDF from there. Files are also kept in the project's `exports` folder.
- **Writing feedback**: in a note, press the check-mark button at the top right (or the same button in **LaTeX** for the open `.tex` file). Quick checks run at once, offline: very long sentences and paragraphs, repeated words, vague or absolute words ("very", "obviously", "proves"), and sentences that report what studies found or give a figure without a citation (`[@key]`, `\cite{}` or "(Author, 2020)" count). **Review with AI** adds a closer read for clarity, structure, uncited claims and grammar. Each point quotes the words it is about, which are underlined in the note; click a point to jump to them. The feedback says what the problem is and asks a question; it never gives replacement wording (any wording the model slips in is removed), so the fix stays yours. Filter by kind with the pills, dismiss points with ×, and press the refresh button after you've revised. The quick word checks are for English; the AI review works in any language.
- **Checking against your sources**: in the same panel, switch to **Sources**. It compares your note (or `.tex` file) with the text of every source in the project and lists passages that use a source's exact words (eight or more in a row) or follow one of its sentences closely with a few words changed or moved. Each shows the source and page, so you can open it, and says whether it has a citation. Text in quotation marks or in a blockquote counts as quoted: those are under **Quoted**, with a note when a quote has no citation. The number at the top is the share of your words in unquoted matches; click a source to see only its matches. It runs on your computer and only knows your project's sources, so it isn't a full plagiarism check, and it never suggests new wording.
- **Thesis plan**: open **Thesis** in the sidebar. **Start from a standard outline** adds Introduction, Literature review, Methodology, Results, Discussion and Conclusion with a share of a 15,000-word goal each, or add your own chapters. Click the word goal at the top or on a chapter to change it, and set your submission date; Folio shows the days left and roughly how many words a day you need. Under each chapter, **Link a note or LaTeX file** points it at the note or `.tex` file you write it in (or makes a new note for it), and its words are counted for you: prose only, without citations, maths, markup or LaTeX commands. The circle on the left sets the chapter's state (not started, drafting, revising, done), and each chapter can have a due date, shown in red when it's late. **Supervisor milestones** is a checklist with dates for things like sending a draft or ethics approval. The top line shows the words you've added today and this week. The chat knows your plan and dates, so it can help you plan your time. Everything is saved in `thesis.json` in the project folder.
- **Literature matrix**: open **Literature matrix** in the sidebar. Each source is a row; the columns start as Research question, Method, Sample / data, Key findings, Limitations and Gaps / future work. Add your own with **+** (say "Theory" or "Country", with a hint of what to look for), and rename or remove them from a column's `…` menu. **Fill empty cells** has the AI take short notes for every empty cell from your highlights (and from the source's text when you've highlighted little), with a page chip after each point: yellow chips are your highlights, and clicking one opens the paper at that page. A cell says "Not found" when the source doesn't cover it. Click any cell to write or correct it; the AI never changes a cell you wrote, and **Fill again** redoes one cell. A row's `…` menu fills just that source or hides it. **Export** copies the matrix as a Markdown table (paste it into a note) or downloads a CSV for Excel, with each source's citation key. The notes are for you to compare sources; the comparison in your review is yours to write.
- **Flashcards**: open **Flashcards** in the sidebar to study what you highlighted. **Make cards** and a source has the AI write one question for each text highlight; the answer is your highlight itself, with its page. **New card** lets you write your own, with the answer typed or taken from a highlight. **Study** shows the cards that are due: think of the answer, press Space to see it, then rate yourself **Again**, **Hard**, **Good** or **Easy** (keys 1 to 4). Cards you know come back after longer and longer gaps; **Again** brings one back in ten minutes. Edit, pause or delete a card from its `…` menu, and click its source to open the page.
- **Supervisor feedback**: open **Supervisor feedback** in the sidebar and import the draft your supervisor sent back, as Word (.docx) or PDF. Every comment becomes a to-do: Word comments with the words they were attached to, tracked changes as "Suggested edit" (insertions, deletions and replacements), and PDF sticky notes and highlights with their page. Tick a comment when you've dealt with it, write a short reply saying what you changed, or choose **Won't change** from its `…` menu and say why. **Find in my notes** opens the note that has the commented words. The bar shows how many are resolved, **Open/All** hides the finished ones, and each new draft you import is kept as its own round. The round's `…` menu copies your replies as a table to paste into an email or note, and opens the original file. The chat knows which comments are still open, so you can ask it how to approach one; it will help you think, not rewrite the passage.
- **Finding papers**: open **Find papers** in the sidebar and search by topic, title, author or DOI. By default it searches OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and arXiv together (the same paper found twice is shown once); the menu next to the search box picks one of them or Crossref, and the filter button limits the years or shows only papers with a free PDF. Click a title to read its abstract. **Add with PDF** puts the paper in your References and downloads its free, legal open-access copy (from arXiv, the publisher or a repository, found through OpenAlex's Unpaywall data and Semantic Scholar) into Sources; **Add** adds only the reference when there is no free copy. If a "free" link turns out to need a login, Folio says so and keeps the reference. The `…` menu on each result shows **Related papers**, **Papers citing this** and **Its references**, so you can follow a paper's trail (the arrow goes back), and **Look for a free PDF**. From **References**, `…` → **Find related papers** starts from a paper you already have. All of this needs the internet; no account or key is needed.
- **Reading list**: open **Reading list** in the sidebar. **Add** puts a source under **To read**, and papers you add from **Find papers** land there by themselves. Put them in order from a row's `…` menu (**Read next**, **Move up**, **Move down**). When you open a PDF on the list and turn past the first page, it moves to **Reading now**, with a bar for how far you got and about how long is left (at about 200 words a minute). Press **Done** when you finish it. Folio also remembers the page you were on in every PDF and opens it there next time.
- **Figures and tables**: open **Figures and tables** in the sidebar. **Add data** takes a CSV file (in Excel: File → Save As → CSV) or cells you paste from a spreadsheet, with the column names in the first row. Folio makes a first chart from it; pick **Bar**, **Line**, **Scatter**, **Table** or **Summary** (N, mean, SD, min, median and max of each number column, optionally per group), the X and Y columns, how to combine rows that share an X value, the axis labels and a caption. Charts use colours that work for colour-blind readers and print well, on a white background. **Add to note** puts the figure (or the table, as a real table) at the end of a note; if you change the figure later, the note shows the new version. **Copy for LaTeX** saves the picture as `latex/figures/<name>.png` and copies a `figure` environment to paste into main.tex (tables become a `tabular`). The `…` menu downloads a PNG or SVG, or a CSV of a table. Figures in notes go into Word and PDF exports too.
- **References and citations**: open **References** in the sidebar and press **Add**. Paste a DOI or doi.org link, BibTeX or RIS (several at once is fine), or type a title to search Crossref and pick the match; you can also import a .bib or .ris file. PDFs you upload that print their DOI are added automatically and linked to the paper. Each reference gets a key like `@walker2002practice`; edit any field, or delete it (Undo brings it back). In a note, **Cite** on the toolbar inserts `[@walker2002practice]` (cite again right after to get `[@a; @b]`), pandoc's citation syntax, so pandoc or Quarto can turn the note into a Word or LaTeX document with real citations. Tick the boxes next to references to pick some: a bar appears where you choose a style and copy them as a reference list or an in-text citation, export them, or delete them. **Export** builds a bibliography for the whole library or just what one note cites, in any of the 10,000+ CSL styles (APA, MLA, Chicago, IEEE, Harvard, Vancouver, or search a journal's name), copied with formatting for Word or Google Docs, or as BibTeX, RIS or CSL JSON. DOI lookup, title search and less common styles need the internet; styles are cached once fetched.
- **LaTeX**: open **LaTeX** in the sidebar to write your paper in LaTeX, with the PDF beside it. Folio compiles on your computer. If you have MacTeX, TeX Live or MiKTeX, it uses that; otherwise it offers to download [Tectonic](https://tectonic-typesetting.github.io), a small LaTeX engine (about 20 MB). The first Tectonic compile downloads the packages your paper uses, so it needs the internet and takes a minute; after that it works offline. The PDF updates a moment after you stop typing (turn this off in the menu next to **Compile**), and `Ctrl/⌘ S` compiles right away. Type `\cite` (or press **Cite**) to pick from your References; `references.bib` is rewritten from them on every compile, so remove the `%` in front of `\bibliography{references}` once you cite something. Errors and warnings appear under the editor; click one to jump to its line. To find a line in the PDF, `Ctrl/⌘`-click it (or press the target button at the top), and the place shows up highlighted in the PDF. Double-click anywhere in the PDF to jump to the line that made it, even in another chapter file. This is SyncTeX; it uses the last compile, so compile after big changes. The file menu at the top adds chapter files (include them with `\input{name}`) and uploads figures (click an image there to insert it). **Download PDF** is in the menu next to **Compile**. For Arabic and other right-to-left languages, use `fontspec` or `polyglossia`: Tectonic handles them, and with TeX Live Folio switches to XeLaTeX by itself. The chat can read the file you have open, so you can ask for feedback on it. **Template** at the top starts main.tex from a journal's template (with a preview of each first page): IEEE (journals and conferences), ACM (journals and conferences), Springer LNCS, Physical Review (APS), AIP, MNRAS, AAS (ApJ), ACS, AMS, Nature, Elsevier, Oxford University Press, APA 7th, or a plain article. They all use LaTeX classes that come with TeX and Tectonic, so they work offline. What you had in main.tex is kept as a separate file (for example `main-before-ieee-journal.tex`), so copy your text across. If your journal isn't listed, download its LaTeX template from the journal's website and upload its `.cls` file from the file menu.
- **Search** (`Ctrl/⌘ K`): finds words in the full text of every paper and note, with the matching passage, and opens the paper at that page. It runs on your computer, so it works offline and without an API key.
- **Themes**: **Settings → Theme** picks the color theme. Folio (warm paper, oxblood ink) is the default; Neutral and 42 presets from [tweakcn](https://tweakcn.com) are built in. To use your own, design it on tweakcn, open **Code**, copy the CSS and press **Paste theme**. Light, dark and system mode work with every theme. Themes that use web fonts load them from Google Fonts when online.
- **Chat** (`Ctrl/⌘ J`): pick any model from your connected providers. The assistant can see the note you have open (click the note chip to hide it). It also searches your papers for passages that match your question, answers from them, and cites them as small chips: click one to read the passage and open the PDF on that page. The papers chip under the box picks which papers it searches. It follows one firm rule in every model: it won't write your paper.
- **Study music**: the player above **Settings** in the sidebar plays lofi hip hop and classical music from YouTube. Press play, or open the list button for other stations and the volume. Paste any YouTube video or playlist link there to add your own. Music keeps playing when you switch views or projects. It needs the internet, and if a live stream has ended Folio tries a backup stream.
- **Pomodoro timer**: the timer sits above the music player. Press play for 25 minutes of focus, then take a 5 minute break, with a 15 minute break after every 4 focus sessions. The tab's title shows the time left, and a chime and a message tell you when it's time to switch. The timer button opens the settings: pick a phase, restart or skip, change the minutes, start the next one by itself, turn the chime off, or pause the music during breaks. It counts your focus sessions for the day and keeps running when you switch views or projects.
